CorsWhy

Describe the request your page makes and the headers the server sent back. CorsWhy says whether a browser would allow it, which check fails first, and the header that fixes it.
Rules follow the Fetch Standard's CORS-safelisted method and header definitions (fetched and read) and the MDN pages on CORS and the Access-Control-Allow-* headers. A preflight is sent when the method is not GET, HEAD or POST, or any header is not CORS-safelisted (Content-Type other than form-urlencoded, multipart/form-data or text/plain, any custom header, Authorization, and so on). Not modelled: redirects, Access-Control-Max-Age caching and browser caps, Access-Control-Expose-Headers, Private Network Access, and the forbidden headers the browser sets itself. The preflight status rule (any 2xx) is the Fetch Standard's rule as I know it; that part of the spec was cut off in the fetch and not read directly. Your own tooling (curl -i with an Origin header) shows the real headers. Runs in your browser, nothing is sent anywhere.
